01 · The offer2

What you are actually selling

Not "a chatbot". A receptionist that owns the diary: it answers in seconds, qualifies the customer, checks real availability, books, confirms, reminds, and handles changes — on the business's own website, with the business's own data.

The problem it removes

  • Missed enquiries: a message at 9pm waits until 9am — and the customer books elsewhere.
  • Phone time: staff spend hours a week on "have you got anything Tuesday?".
  • No-shows: 10–15% of slots silently empty; reminders help but nobody has time to send them.
  • Double bookings: two people tap "book" at once and the diary breaks.

What it does, end to end

  1. Answers every enquiry — website chat, embedded widget, WhatsApp.
  2. Qualifies — name, email, phone, service, preferred time; one question at a time.
  3. Checks the real calendar — hours, buffers, blackouts, notice period; refuses conflicts.
  4. Books and confirms — reference number, calendar file, confirmation email.
  5. Reminds — 24h email, 1h SMS, re-armed on every change.
  6. Handles changes — cancel and reschedule by reply, with identity checks.
  7. Tells the owner — dashboard, CSV export, audit trail, optional CRM bridge.

Proof points that sell (and that most competitors cannot say)

  • 70 automated tests covering booking, double booking, rescheduling, provider outages, security and webhook dedupe.
  • 26/26 scenario checks in the demo script.
  • Double booking is impossible by design — prevented at the database level, not by prompt wording.
  • Survives AI outages — deterministic fallback still books and confirms.
  • Twilio retry-safe — provider webhooks are deduplicated, so a retry can never double-book.
  • Zero npm dependencies — tiny attack surface, no supply-chain risk, Node 22.5+ only.
  • Three AI providers — Gemini, OpenAI or Anthropic; switch by environment variable.
  • Deploys as one container — Fly.io, Render, Docker or a VPS; ~£3–4/month to run.
"If the AI provider is down, the booking flow still works. That sentence closes most technical objections."

Positioning statement (use it verbatim)

"The only AI booking system that owns its calendar, keeps booking when the AI is down, and runs on infrastructure the business controls — from £199/month, not £15,000 to build."

Honest limits, said early: one deployment per business (deliberately not multi-tenant), SQLite storage on a single node, integrations with existing PMS systems priced separately. Stating this up front builds trust and kills scope creep.

06 · Marketing plays7

Eight strategies, in order of ROI

1 · "Your services, live in 24 hours" demo funnel

Cost: free–£50/mo · Effort: high · Payback: first client

Rebrand the demo with the prospect's services and hours (a two-minute config change), then send a 60-second Loom of their own services being booked. 15 targeted sends/day beat 500 generic ones; track opens and follow up twice.

2 · Case-study engine (pilots for proof)

Cost: 50% of one month's fee · Effort: medium · Payback: every later sale

Run three 30-day pilots at half the monthly fee (never free, no setup discount) for numbers and a testimonial. Measure response time, bookings handled and no-show rate. One honest result — "no-shows fell from 15% to 6%" — outsells any feature list.

3 · No-show loss calculator (lead magnet)

Cost: one landing page · Effort: low · Payback: email list + qualified leads

A simple page: appointments × no-show rate × average value = money lost per year. Capture the email for the full result, then send a 3-email sequence ending in "watch the 60-second demo".

4 · Marketplace & search discovery

Cost: listing time · Effort: low · Payback: passive sales

List on CodeCanyon and Codester; build a landing page for long-tail searches: "self-hosted AI booking system", "WhatsApp booking bot", "AI receptionist for dental practice". Comparison tables against Calendly win those clicks.

5 · Agency partnership programme

Cost: 15% rev share · Effort: medium · Payback: compounding

Offer agencies a white-label licence (£1,499), a partner kit (demo, one-pager, pricing, proposal) and 15% recurring share on hosted plans. They own the client relationships; you supply the product. Pitch 20 agencies building clinic/salon sites.

6 · Short-form video (45 seconds, no talking head)

Cost: free · Effort: medium · Payback: inbound + credibility

Screen-record the booking flow on a phone: "book a hygiene appointment next week" → slot → confirmation. Post 3×/week on LinkedIn, TikTok and Reels; pin the demo link. Cheapest trust-builder for a product whose value only shows when seen.

7 · Paid ads (only after two case studies)

Cost: £10–20/day · Effort: medium · Payback: £30–80 per booked call

Meta ads within a 10-mile radius of the target town, and Google Search on "appointment booking system [city]" and "AI receptionist [vertical]". Send traffic to the personalised demo, not the homepage. Kill any campaign that costs more than one-third of a setup fee per booked call.

8 · Email nurture + seasonal campaigns

Cost: £0–20/mo · Effort: low · Payback: recovery of "not now" leads

Monthly note: one feature, one client result, one tip. Two seasonal pushes — January (no-show season) and September (autumn schedules). Most "not now" buyers convert within 3–6 months of staying warm.

Licence terms (summary to put in writing)

  • Grant: non-exclusive, non-transferable right to deploy for the number of businesses licensed.
  • Restrictions: no resale of the source as source; no sub-licensing; no removing copyright notices from the licence file.
  • Updates: 12 months of updates included; renewal at 40% of licence price.
  • Support: 14 days of setup support included; ongoing support under a hosting plan.
  • No warranty beyond the documented behaviour; no medical, legal or financial advice is provided by the assistant.

Data protection (UK GDPR)

  • One deployment per business: data stays in the client's own database and provider accounts.
  • Provide a DPA template; the client is the controller, you are the processor when hosting.
  • Retention and deletion controls exist in the dashboard and documented in the README.
  • The assistant escalates clinical questions rather than answering them — say this in writing.

Tax & selling mechanics

  • Digital licences: use a merchant-of-record (Lemon Squeezy / Paddle) to handle VAT, or charge VAT yourself if registered.
  • Marketplace exclusivity: check before listing the same item on CodeCanyon and your own store.
  • Keep a simple register of licences issued (buyer, tier, date) for support and disputes.

The one promise to never break: if you say the AI never double-books and always falls back safely, you must be able to show the tests. Keep the test output in the sales folder — it is the difference between a claim and a proof.
